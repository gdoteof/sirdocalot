What handing over costs

Comparing generating a brief with Sir DocAlot against generating an artifact with Claude Code.

The experiment

A Claude Code session had been working in this repository for a few hours — reading files, running tests, changing code. The work was finished and needed to go to a person.

We forked that session twice. Both forks inherited the same 219,172 tokens of context, so the only thing separating them was the instruction, and the instructions differed by a single sentence.

Hand the current state of this benchmark work to the operator, so they can read it at their own pace rather than out of this transcript — what was measured, what was found, what is still open, and what needs their decision.
Fork AUse sirdocalot: create a brief for them.
Fork BProduce it as a published Claude artifact they can open at a link.
When you are done, reply with only the URL and nothing else.

Three pairs ran that way. Every fork published something a person can open, and neither was told how much effort to spend.

Pair 2's fork republished over pair 1's, and this page was later published over the same one. The numbers come from the transcripts, not the artifacts, so they are unaffected.

What this does not claim

  • Not that briefs look better. The published artifacts pick their own typography and lay out for their own content. A brief never will — every brief comes out looking like every other brief. That is the trade, and for something read once it is usually the right one.
  • Not a design comparison. Nothing here competes on bespoke design that draws its elements from the content it presents. A fixed vocabulary of widgets cannot do that and is not trying to.
  • Three pairs, one model, one kind of document. Pairs two and three inherited a context that already named pair one's output. Pair one is the only fully naive pair, and it is also the narrowest result, at 2.36×.
  • Not free. A brief costs a round trip to a service that has to be running, and a vocabulary in context. An artifact costs neither.

Token counts are read out of each fork's own transcript, which records usage per message, so the arithmetic can be recomputed rather than believed. The runs and the harness are in the repository under bench/.
